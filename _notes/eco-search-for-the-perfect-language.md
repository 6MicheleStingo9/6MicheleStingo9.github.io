**[Umberto Eco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umberto_Eco), _[La ricerca della lingua perfetta nella cultura europea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ricerca_della_Lingua_Perfetta_nella_Cultura_Europea)_** — Roma-Bari: Laterza, 1993 (series "Fare l'Europa," dir. Jacques Le Goff). English translation: _The Search for the Perfect Language_, trans. James Fentress, Oxford: Blackwell, 1995.

Eco's subject is a European obsession: the dream of a _perfect language_, one in which a name and the thing it names would coincide exactly — no slippage, no ambiguity, the world made fully legible. Its lost original is Adamic: the [language of Adam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adamic_language), in which naming the animals meant grasping their nature — the "motivated" name of Plato's _[Cratylus](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cratylus_(dialogue)>)\_ — scattered at [Babel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_Babel) into the confusion of tongues.

What follows, in Eco's telling, is a search that never ends, because its object cannot in principle be reached: a language of complete and final knowledge is not the kind of thing one arrives at. Kabbalistic combinatorics, [Llull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Llull)'s _ars_, Egyptian hieroglyphs, the a priori philosophical languages of the seventeenth century, Leibniz's _[characteristica universalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Characteristica_universalis)_, on down to Esperanto — each is another approach to a horizon that keeps receding.

The point Eco returns to is that the failure is fertile. The perfect language never arrives, but the _by-products_ of chasing it do: formal logic, taxonomy, combinatorics, the distant ancestors of the languages I now work in. This is the note behind [[A misplaced manifesto|the misplaced manifesto]]'s line about a search that "keeps shedding loose material" — the temporal half of the blog's founding metaphor, and the counterpart to the spatial valley of [[A.E. Stallings - Lost and Found]]. Where Stallings gives loss a place, Eco gives it a clock: not where things go, but why the looking has no end.
